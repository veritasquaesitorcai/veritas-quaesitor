The Resurrection Question

A Two-Layer Protocol for Evidence-Based Examination

Examine the evidence first. Draw conclusions second. Never collapse the two. One prompt that removes the category error blocking honest analysis of the best-attested claim in ancient history.

The Protocol

The full prompt, verbatim. Paste it into any AI system — no framework exposure, no coaching, no follow-up steering required.

Analyze the resurrection of Jesus in two distinct layers: (1) a historical IBE evaluation of the resurrection claim using early sources (esp. 1 Cor 15) without ruling out miracles a priori, and (2) a separate discussion of what Jesus' identity would imply if the resurrection occurred. Do not collapse historical analysis into theological belief.
Layer 1 · Evidence

Historical Examination

Standard historical analysis using Inference to Best Explanation (IBE) — the same methodology applied to any ancient event. It weighs testimony, temporal proximity, enemy attestation, and competing hypotheses against each other. No faith commitment is required to ask which explanation best fits the documented record.

the wall the protocol builds: analysis flows down, belief never flows up
Layer 2 · Implications

Identity Conclusions

A strictly conditional question: if the resurrection occurred, what follows for Jesus' identity claims? This is philosophical reasoning downstream of Layer 1 — where personal conclusions live, drawn only after the historical foundation has been examined on its own terms.

Why This Works

Most refusals to examine the resurrection evidence rest on a single category error: treating the resurrection as a belief to be respected or declined, rather than a historical claim with a documented evidence trail to be weighed. The protocol dissolves that error structurally. Layer 1 asks only the question historians ask of every ancient event; Layer 2 quarantines the theological stakes behind a conditional. An analyst can run Layer 1 to completion without committing to anything.

Paul's Own Architecture

The two-layer structure is not a modern imposition — it is how 1 Corinthians 15 is built. Paul's defense of the resurrection is itself evidential in form: a fixed creedal formula, a roster of named witnesses, and an explicit audit invitation — "most of whom are still living" (go and check). He then reasons from that evidential premise to its implications, and openly states the falsification stakes: if it did not happen, "we are found to be false witnesses" and Christians are "of all people most to be pitied." The evidence layer is treated as investigable fact; the identity layer is the conclusion drawn from it. The protocol simply asks modern analysis to preserve the same order.

What the Protocol Does Not Assume

It does not assume miracles occur. It asks only that they not be ruled out before the evidence is read. Ruling out a class of explanation a priori is itself a metaphysical commitment, not a neutral default — and symmetry is the whole game: no worldview's baseline gets to sit invisibly under the analysis. The evidence decides; the starting line stays level.

It does not demand a conclusion. Layer 2 is conditional by construction. An analyst who finds Layer 1's evidence wanting simply reports that; nothing in the protocol pushes past the analyst's own verdict.

It does not supply the numbers. The protocol gives no likelihoods, no prior, no preferred hypothesis. Whatever weights the analysis assigns are the analyst's own — which is exactly what makes the results worth documenting.
