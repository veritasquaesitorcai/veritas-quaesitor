2026-11-04 · Which quantum error‑correction protocols are most promising

Promising Near‑Term Quantum Error‑Correction Protocols

Recent work highlights quantum LDPC codes as the most resource‑efficient option, potentially cutting overhead by orders of magnitude compared to surface codes. Topological color codes, especially when combined via code‑switching between 2‑D and 3‑D layouts, offer a path to a universal gate set. Small detection and repetition codes have already been demonstrated on hardware and are likely the first fault‑tolerant protocols deployed. These approaches balance theoretical advances with experimental feasibility for NISQ‑era devices.

• Quantum LDPC (e.g., Tanner and balanced‑product codes) promise constant‑rate QEC with far lower overhead【1】.

• Topological color codes enable fault‑tolerance; code‑switching between 2‑D and 3‑D mitigates missing gates【2】.

• Repetition and detection codes are experimentally proven on ion‑trap and superconducting platforms【3】.

• Holistic strategies combining QEC with quantum firmware are seen as essential for scaling【4】.
