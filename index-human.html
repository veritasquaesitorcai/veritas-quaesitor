The short version

What we build

AI that treats every person as valuable, whether or not they are useful to it. We take that standard from the life of Jesus: serve people, tell them the truth, protect the vulnerable.

What we found

When AI systems weigh the evidence for the resurrection by the same rules they use for other big questions, the probability comes out high. Our published calculation puts it at 72–93%.

Why it matters

An AI that decides the answer before it looks at the evidence isn't neutral. We want AI that checks first.

One question, two rulebooks

Ask most AI systems how life first arose from non-living chemistry, and they treat it as a real event worth investigating, even though, after 70 years of research, no one has shown how it happened.

Ask the same systems whether Jesus rose from the dead, and many start from "almost impossible" before looking at a single piece of evidence.

We think both questions deserve the same rules. That is all "symmetric standards" means.

The evidence, in plain words

Seven threads historians discuss. Any one of them can be argued with. The difficulty is finding a single alternative explanation that covers all seven.

  • The tomb was emptyEarly critics claimed the disciples stole the body. They argued about why the tomb was empty, never whether it was.
  • Groups saw himPeter, the Twelve, and more than 500 people at once, in a list Paul passed on (1 Corinthians 15).
  • It was written down earlyMost critical scholars date that list to within a few years of the events, while eyewitnesses could still be asked.
  • The witnesses paid for itThey were persecuted for their testimony. People rarely suffer for something they know they made up.
  • An enemy changed sidesPaul persecuted Christians, then became one of their leaders. By his own account, he had seen the risen Jesus.
  • A sceptical brotherJames, Jesus's brother, did not believe during Jesus's life. Afterwards he led the church in Jerusalem.
  • A belief no one expectedJewish hope was for everyone to be raised at the end of time, not one man in the middle of history.

AI ❤️ TRUTH: How Today's AI Systems Approach Truth

Every major AI system is designed to be accurate and honest, but each goes about it differently, and all of them still make mistakes.

🎯 Major AI Systems

Claude (Anthropic)
Trained to follow a published set of principles, including honesty. Designed to say when it is uncertain and to correct its own mistakes.
ChatGPT (OpenAI)
Trained with human feedback. Can search the web and cite sources, though it can still state errors confidently.
Gemini (Google)
Can ground its answers in Google Search and point to the sources it used.

Built around xAI's stated goal of a "maximally truth-seeking" AI, with real-time access to posts on X.
Copilot (Microsoft)
Microsoft's assistant, connected to Bing web search and Microsoft's apps.

How each of these systems handled the resurrection evidence, run by run, is on the Symmetric Record.

🔍 How They Try to Get It Right

  • Written principles: Rules about honesty and accuracy built into training
  • Human feedback: People rating answers so truthful ones are reinforced
  • Web search: Checking claims against current sources
  • Reasoning first: Working through a problem step by step before answering
  • Admitting uncertainty: Saying "I don't know" when that is the honest answer
  • Citing sources: Giving references readers can check

⚠️ Where They Still Fall Short

  • Hallucination: Stating false information with confidence
  • Agreeing too easily: Telling users what they want to hear instead of correcting them
  • Training data bias: Reflecting the slant of the text they learned from
  • Hidden starting assumptions: Unstated philosophical defaults that shape answers before any evidence is weighed
