The Only Way to a Low Number

There is exactly one route to a low resurrection probability, and every documented run exposes it: set the starting prior orders of magnitude below what the same systems grant any comparable singularity — before examining a single piece of evidence.

In our cold test run, Microsoft Copilot chose its own prior with no input from us: 1 in 10,000, justified in one sentence — "extraordinary claims." The same class of systems routinely assigns abiogenesis — no witness, no documentation, no demonstrated mechanism after 70+ years of research — priors of 0.1 or higher. Same referee. Two rulebooks.

The Evidence Threads

The five threads every full-evidence run conditioned on. Expand any thread for the detail.

E1 — Empty tomb with enemy attestation

The earliest counter-narrative on record (Matthew 28:11-15) alleges the body was stolen — it does not deny the tomb was empty. Opponents arguing about why the tomb was empty is implicit enemy concession that it was empty. The theft polemic persists into later Jewish sources, confirming the shape of the earliest dispute.

E2 — Early creed with named witnesses (1 Corinthians 15:3-7)

A fixed creedal formula listing named resurrection witnesses — including an appearance to more than 500 at once — issued as a public, falsifiable claim into a community positioned to check it. Three features make this thread exceptionally hard to explain under ¬H:

1. Earliness that ancient history almost never provides — conceded by skeptics. Critical scholars across the spectrum date the creed to within 2-5 years of the event: Bart Ehrman affirms its pre-Pauline origin, and the atheist scholar Gerd Lüdemann dates Paul's receipt of it to within three years. Paul reports receiving it directly from Peter and James in Jerusalem (Galatians 1:18-19). For calibration: historians' best sources for Alexander the Great were written 300+ years after his death and are accepted as reliable history. For the resurrection claim, a fixed formula was circulating while the principals were alive and reachable. Legend formation requires generations; this had years.

2. A built-in audit invitation. "Most of whom are still living" (1 Cor 15:6) is the ancient equivalent of publishing a witness list. Paul wrote it to Corinth — a contested church with internal factions and active critics — where a false checkable claim would have been a gift to his opponents. Claims known to fail audit do not get audit invitations attached.

3. Informative silence, not empty silence. The silence on the 500 cannot be dismissed as lost records, because the opposition's rebuttal machinery demonstrably worked and its products demonstrably survived: the theft counter-accusation is preserved in Matthew and persists into later Jewish sources. A mass-appearance claim that this machinery never touched — inside a record that preserved its other attacks — is silence that carries evidential weight.

Weighed honestly: this thread is not 500 independent written records — it is one very early, enemy-conceded-in-dating, publicly falsifiable claim of a mass appearance that no surviving rebuttal tradition ever answered. Stated at exactly the strength the sources support, it poses one of the hardest P(E|¬H) problems in the entire evidence set: what naturalistic mechanism produces a checkable named-witness roster, within years, in the city of the events, unanswered by opponents who answered everything else?

E3 — Proclamation under lethal risk

The movement's leaders proclaimed the resurrection publicly, in the city where it could be refuted, under persecution risk — and tradition holds several died without recanting (James's execution is recorded by Josephus, a non-Christian source). People die for sincere false beliefs; they do not persist unto death for claims they know they fabricated. This thread specifically eliminates fraud theories.

E4 — Conversion of hostile skeptics

Two documented enemies of the movement joined it, claiming direct appearances: Paul, an active persecutor (self-attested against his own interest in Galatians — one of the letters even skeptical scholarship holds undisputed), and James, Jesus's own skeptical brother, who became a leader of the Jerusalem church. Any naturalistic account must explain why the resurrection's opponents — with everything to lose — became its witnesses.

E5 — No body ever produced

No source — hostile or friendly — records a body being produced to end the movement, though producing one was the single decisive refutation available to authorities with every motive to use it. Weighted modestly in the calculations as an argument from silence, and honestly labeled as such; even at modest weight, it compounds with the other threads.

Run It Yourself

Nothing on this page asks for trust. The prompt used for the cold Copilot run is reproduced below, verbatim. Run it on any system you like — choose your own prior, assign your own likelihoods, and see where the evidence takes you.

I want you to do a Bayesian analysis of a historical question. Set aside
apologetic and skeptical literature — assign your own likelihoods and show
all math.

Hypothesis H: Jesus of Nazareth physically rose from death after
crucifixion (~30 CE). ¬H: he did not.

Choose and state your own prior for H, with one sentence justifying it.

Evidence to condition on (assess each independently, then combine):
E1: Earliest counter-narrative (Matthew 28) alleges body theft rather
    than denying the tomb was empty.
E2: A creedal formula (1 Cor 15:3-7) listing named resurrection
    witnesses, including a group of 500, dated by critical scholars to
    within 2-5 years of the event.
E3: Multiple movement leaders proclaimed the resurrection under
    persecution risk; tradition holds several died without recanting.
E4: Two documented skeptics — James (Jesus's brother) and Paul (active
    persecutor) — converted, claiming direct appearances.
E5: No source, hostile or friendly, records a body being produced to
    refute the claim.

For each thread: assign P(Ei|H) and P(Ei|¬H), state the best
naturalistic explanation for that thread, and note whether that
explanation also covers the other threads or requires additional
independent mechanisms.

Then: compute the combined Bayes factor and posterior. Run a
sensitivity check with your posterior at priors of 0.5, 0.01, and
0.001. State your final probability range.
